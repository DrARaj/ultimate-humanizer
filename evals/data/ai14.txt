The Hanseatic League was a commercial and defensive confederation of merchant guilds and market towns that dominated Northern European trade from the late 13th to the 17th century. Growing out of earlier associations of German merchants, it eventually included more than 170 cities, stretching from the Baltic to the North Sea and as far as Novgorod and London. Lübeck served as its de facto leader. The League secured trading privileges, maintained foreign trading posts (Kontore) in Bruges, Bergen, London, and Novgorod, and sometimes raised fleets to protect shipping or wage war. Its main exports included timber, grain, furs, and fish, while it imported cloth, wine, and salt. Decline came with the rise of territorial states, the shift of trade toward the Atlantic, and the Thirty Years' War; by the 1660s the League had effectively ended.
