Insulin is a peptide hormone produced by the beta cells of the pancreatic islets of Langerhans and is the principal regulator of blood glucose. Secreted when blood glucose rises, typically after a meal, it signals muscle, adipose, and liver cells to take up glucose, store it as glycogen or fat, and suppress hepatic glucose production. It binds the insulin receptor, a receptor tyrosine kinase, which triggers the translocation of GLUT4 transporters to the cell surface in muscle and fat tissue. Insulin also promotes protein synthesis and lipid storage. Frederick Banting and Charles Best isolated it at the University of Toronto in 1921, transforming the treatment of diabetes mellitus. Deficient insulin production, as in type 1 diabetes, or resistance to its effects, as in type 2 diabetes, leads to chronically elevated blood glucose. Insulin was also the first protein whose amino acid sequence was determined, by Frederick Sanger in 1951.
