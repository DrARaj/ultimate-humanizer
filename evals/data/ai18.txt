The Byzantine Empire was the continuation of the Eastern Roman Empire, centred on Constantinople (modern Istanbul), which survived about a thousand years after the western half of Rome collapsed in 476 CE. The name is a later coinage; its inhabitants called themselves Romans. Under Justinian I, the empire reconquered parts of Italy and North Africa and codified Roman law in the *Corpus Juris Civilis*. Its position astride trade routes between Europe and Asia made Constantinople one of the wealthiest cities of the medieval world, and Byzantine culture profoundly shaped Orthodox Christianity, art, and architecture. The empire endured repeated crises, including Arab sieges in the seventh century, Bulgarian and Seljuk wars, and the sack of Constantinople by the Fourth Crusade in 1204. It partially recovered but was weakened by civil war and the Ottoman advance. Constantinople fell to the Ottoman Turks under Mehmed II in 1453, ending the empire.
