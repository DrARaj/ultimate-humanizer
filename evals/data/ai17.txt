The Industrial Revolution was a period of profound economic and social transformation that began in Great Britain in the late eighteenth century and spread across Europe and North America in the following decades. Its key drivers included improved steam power, the mechanization of textile production, and the growing use of coal and iron in manufacturing and transport. Factory-based production replaced much home and workshop manufacturing, drawing workers into rapidly expanding cities and creating new social classes of industrial capitalists and wage laborers. Railways and steamships dramatically reduced the cost and time of moving goods and people, while agricultural advances supported growing populations. Although the era ultimately raised living standards and expanded global trade, it also brought harsh working conditions, child labor, urban overcrowding, and pollution, which in turn spurred reforms in labor law, public health, and education.
