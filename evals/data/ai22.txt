The Danube is the second-longest river in Europe, after the Volga, flowing roughly 2,850 kilometres from its source in the Black Forest of southwestern Germany to the Black Sea. Its drainage basin covers about 817,000 square kilometres and spans ten countries, more than any other river in the world. Flowing eastward, it passes through or along four capital cities, Vienna, Bratislava, Budapest and Belgrade, and forms national borders in several stretches. Historically, the river marked the northern frontier of the Roman Empire, and its banks hosted important settlements such as Vindobona and Aquincum. Today it is a major commercial waterway, linked to the Rhine–Main–Danube canal, which connects it to the North Sea. Its lower course ends in the Danube Delta, one of the largest and best-preserved wetlands in Europe and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, where the river divides into numerous channels before reaching the Black Sea.
