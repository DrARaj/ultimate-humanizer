Tardigrades, also called water bears, are microscopic animals in the phylum Tardigrada, which contains more than 1,300 described species. Usually 0.1 to 1.5 millimetres long, they have plump bodies and four pairs of stubby legs ending in claws. They live in mosses, lichens, leaf litter, freshwater, and marine sediments worldwide, from the Himalayas to the deep sea. They are best known for surviving extreme conditions. By entering a dehydrated, dormant state called the tun, they can endure desiccation for years, tolerate temperatures near absolute zero and well above boiling, and withstand high doses of ionizing radiation. Some species survived exposure to the vacuum of outer space in orbital experiments. Most feed by piercing plant cells, algae, or small invertebrates with a stylet and sucking out their contents. Phylogenetically, they belong to Panarthropoda, alongside arthropods and velvet worms. Johann August Ephraim Goeze described the first tardigrade in 1773.
