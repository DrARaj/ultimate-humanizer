Tobacco smoking is the practice of burning dried, processed leaves of plants in the genus *Nicotiana*, typically in cigarettes, cigars, or pipes, and inhaling the resulting smoke to absorb nicotine. Indigenous Americans smoked tobacco ceremonially and medicinally long before European contact. The habit spread worldwide after the sixteenth century and became mass-market by the late nineteenth century, when manufactured cigarettes made it widely accessible. Its smoke contains thousands of chemicals, many carcinogenic, and nicotine is highly addictive. Smoking is the leading preventable cause of death worldwide, linked to cancers of the lung, mouth, and throat, as well as to cardiovascular disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and numerous other conditions. Since the 1960s, public health campaigns, advertising restrictions, smoke-free laws, taxation, and cessation aids have reduced smoking prevalence in many high-income countries, although use remains common in much of the world.
