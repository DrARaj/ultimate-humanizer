Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in carbohydrates. In plants, it takes place mainly in chloroplasts, where the pigment chlorophyll absorbs light, chiefly in the blue and red wavelengths. The light-dependent reactions, which occur in the thylakoid membranes, split water molecules, releasing oxygen as a byproduct, and generate ATP and NADPH. These energy carriers then power the Calvin cycle in the stroma, where the enzyme RuBisCO fixes atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic molecules, ultimately producing sugars such as glucose. In simplified form, six molecules each of carbon dioxide and water, combined with light energy, yield one glucose molecule and six molecules of oxygen. Photosynthesis underpins nearly all life on Earth, supplying much of the oxygen breathed by aerobic organisms and forming the base of most food chains. It also helps regulate atmospheric carbon dioxide levels, making it central to the global carbon cycle.
