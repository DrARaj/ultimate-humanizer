Hypertension, commonly called high blood pressure, is a chronic condition in which the force of blood against the arterial walls is persistently elevated. Under current American guidelines, a reading of 130/80 mmHg or higher meets the diagnostic threshold. Roughly 1.28 billion adults worldwide are affected, and many are unaware of it because it usually causes no symptoms, earning it the nickname "silent killer." About 90 to 95 percent of cases are primary (essential) hypertension, which develops gradually from genetic predisposition, aging, obesity, inactivity, high salt intake, and excess alcohol use. Sustained elevation damages blood vessels and is a leading modifiable risk factor for stroke, coronary artery disease, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease. Treatment combines lifestyle changes with medications such as thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, and calcium channel blockers, with the goal of lowering pressure to target levels.
