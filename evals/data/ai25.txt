Madagascar is an island nation in the Indian Ocean, separated from the southeastern coast of Africa by the Mozambique Channel. At about 587,000 square kilometres, it is the world's fourth-largest island. It split from the African mainland more than 100 million years ago, and its long isolation produced one of the planet's most distinctive natural environments. An estimated 90 percent of its plants and animals occur nowhere else on Earth, including all of its lemur species. Landscapes range from eastern rainforests to dry spiny forests in the south and west, and the island is recognised as a global biodiversity hotspot. Madagascar gained independence from France in 1960. Its population of about 30 million is predominantly Malagasy, and Malagasy and French are the official languages. The capital, Antananarivo, is its largest city. Agriculture dominates the economy, and the country is the world's leading producer of vanilla. Despite its natural wealth, Madagascar remains one of the world's poorer nations, facing deforestation and widespread poverty.
