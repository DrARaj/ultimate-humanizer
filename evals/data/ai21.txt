Lake Baikal is a rift lake in southern Siberia, Russia, bordered by the Irkutsk Oblast and the Republic of Buryatia. It is the world's oldest lake, with an estimated age of 25 to 30 million years, and its deepest, reaching about 1,642 metres. It holds roughly a fifth of the planet's unfrozen surface fresh water, making it the largest freshwater lake by volume. Its long isolation has produced exceptional biodiversity, with well over a thousand plant and animal species, most of them found nowhere else. The nerpa, the only freshwater seal, is among its best-known residents. Sediments accumulating on the lake floor preserve a detailed climate record, and the basin continues to widen slowly as the Baikal Rift Zone spreads. Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996, Baikal faces pressures from pollution, industry, and tourism, and ongoing conservation efforts aim to protect its unique ecosystem.
