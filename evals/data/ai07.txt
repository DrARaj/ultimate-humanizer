The cell nucleus is a membrane-bound organelle found in eukaryotic cells that houses most of the cell's genetic material, organized as linear DNA molecules complexed with histone proteins to form chromatin. Typically the largest organelle in the cell, it is enclosed by a double-membrane nuclear envelope perforated by nuclear pore complexes that regulate the exchange of molecules such as RNA and proteins with the cytoplasm. Within the nucleus, the nucleolus serves as the site of ribosomal RNA synthesis and ribosome subunit assembly, while chromatin is organized into regions of varying compaction that influence gene accessibility. The nucleus is the principal site of DNA replication and transcription, and the processing of messenger RNA before its export to the cytoplasm for translation. Its discovery is credited to Robert Brown, who described it in orchid cells in 1831. The nucleus is absent in prokaryotes, whose genetic material resides in a nucleoid region without a surrounding membrane.
