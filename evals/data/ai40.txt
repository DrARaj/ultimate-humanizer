Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is the ability of microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites to survive exposure to drugs designed to kill them or inhibit their growth. It arises through natural genetic mutation and selection, but human activity has greatly accelerated its spread, including the overuse and misuse of antibiotics in medicine, agriculture, and animal husbandry, as well as inadequate infection control and sanitation. Resistance genes can move between microbes through horizontal gene transfer, allowing resistance to spread even among unrelated species. The consequences are severe: infections become harder or impossible to treat, surgery and cancer chemotherapy carry greater risk, and healthcare costs and mortality rise. The World Health Organization identifies AMR as one of the most serious threats to global health and development. Responses include antimicrobial stewardship programs, improved surveillance, vaccination, development of new drugs and diagnostics, and the One Health approach, which recognizes that human, animal, and environmental health are interconnected.
