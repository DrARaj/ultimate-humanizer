Florence Nightingale (1820–1910) was a British nurse, statistician, and social reformer regarded as the founder of modern professional nursing. Born in Florence, Italy, and raised in England, she trained in nursing in Germany and France before rising to prominence during the Crimean War (1853–1856). Her reforms at the British military hospital in Scutari, including improvements to sanitation, ventilation, nutrition, and supply management, dramatically reduced soldiers' mortality. Her statistical analyses, presented through graphics such as the polar area diagram, helped persuade officials that most deaths resulted from preventable disease rather than battle wounds, prompting sweeping reforms in army medical administration. In 1860 she founded the Nightingale Training School for Nurses at St Thomas' Hospital in London, which set lasting standards for nursing education. Her 1859 book *Notes on Nursing* became an influential guide to patient care, and she continued writing on public health and hospital design for decades. She died in London in 1910.
