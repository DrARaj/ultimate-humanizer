The Meiji Restoration was a series of political events in 1868 that ended the Tokugawa shogunate's rule of Japan and restored practical authority to the Emperor Meiji, long a figurehead in Kyoto. Samurai from the southwestern domains of Satsuma, Chōshū, and Tosa, angered by the shogunate's concessions to Western powers, led the movement and defeated loyalist forces in the Boshin War (1868–1869). Under the slogan "rich country, strong army," the new government abolished the feudal domains in 1871, built a centralized bureaucracy and a conscript army, and pursued rapid industrialization and Western-style reforms in law, education, and the economy. The Meiji Constitution of 1889 created a constitutional monarchy with an elected lower house, though sovereignty nominally remained with the emperor. Historians regard the era as the foundation of Japan's emergence as a modern industrial power.
