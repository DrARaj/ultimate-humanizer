The Mali Empire was a West African state traditionally dated to about 1235, when Sundiata Keita defeated the Sosso king Sumanguru Kanté at the Battle of Kirina. At its height in the fourteenth century, it controlled the upper Niger River valley and the trans-Saharan trade networks that carried gold and salt between sub-Saharan Africa, North Africa, and the Mediterranean. Cities such as Timbuktu and Djenné became important centers of Islamic scholarship. Mansa Musa, who ruled around 1312 to 1337, is remembered for his lavish pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 to 1325, which reportedly depressed gold prices in Cairo for roughly a decade. Mali's authority weakened in the fifteenth century amid succession disputes and rebellions, and the Songhai Empire absorbed much of its territory. The modern Republic of Mali takes its name from the historic empire.
