The Black Death was a devastating pandemic caused by the bacterium *Yersinia pestis*, which spread across Eurasia and North Africa between 1346 and 1353. It is thought to have originated in Central Asia and was carried westward along trade routes, reaching the Crimean port of Caffa in 1347 and then Mediterranean Europe through Genoese and Venetian ships. Transmitted mainly by fleas carried on rats and other rodents, and in its pneumonic form directly between people, it produced painful swelling of the lymph nodes known as buboes, along with fever, and it often killed within days. Estimates suggest it killed between 30 and 50 percent of Europe's population, and outbreaks recurred for centuries afterward. The demographic shock produced labor shortages that raised wages and weakened feudal obligations, contributing to social unrest such as the Peasants' Revolt of 1381. The epidemic also prompted widespread persecution of Jewish communities, who were falsely blamed for the disease.
