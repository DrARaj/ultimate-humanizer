Malaria is a life-threatening infectious disease caused by *Plasmodium* parasites, which are transmitted to humans through the bites of infected female *Anopheles* mosquitoes. Five species infect humans, and *Plasmodium falciparum* causes most malaria deaths, particularly among young children in sub-Saharan Africa. After inoculation, parasites multiply first in the liver and then in red blood cells, where cyclical rupture produces the characteristic fever, chills, and sweating. Severe infection can cause anaemia, cerebral malaria, respiratory distress, and organ failure if untreated. Diagnosis relies on microscopy of blood smears or rapid antigen tests, and treatment usually involves artemisinin-based combination therapies. Prevention includes insecticide-treated bed nets, indoor residual spraying, and, more recently, the RTS,S and R21 vaccines. Although historically widespread across tropical and subtropical regions, malaria has been eliminated from many countries, yet it still causes hundreds of thousands of deaths annually according to World Health Organization estimates.
