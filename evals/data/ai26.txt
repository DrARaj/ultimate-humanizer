Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace (10 December 1815 – 27 November 1852), was an English mathematician and writer best known for her work on Charles Babbage's proposed Analytical Engine, a general-purpose mechanical computer that was never completed. Born Ada Byron, the only legitimate child of the poet Lord Byron, she was tutored in mathematics from an early age, including by Augustus De Morgan. In 1843 she translated an article on the engine by Luigi Menabrea and appended extensive notes of her own. Those notes include a procedure for calculating Bernoulli numbers that is widely regarded as the first published algorithm intended for implementation on a machine. She also argued that the engine could manipulate symbols and music, not merely numbers, anticipating modern general-purpose computing. Lovelace died of uterine cancer at the age of 36. The United States Department of Defense named the programming language Ada after her in 1980.
