Mount Kilimanjaro is a dormant stratovolcano in northeastern Tanzania, near the border with Kenya, and the highest mountain in Africa. Its summit, Uhuru Peak on the Kibo cone, rises to about 5,895 metres (19,341 feet) above sea level. Kilimanjaro is often described as the world's highest free-standing mountain, meaning it is not part of a larger range. The massif comprises three volcanic cones: Kibo, the youngest and tallest; Mawenzi, a more eroded and rugged peak; and Shira, the oldest, now largely collapsed. Its upper slopes once held glaciers and ice fields, which have shrunk dramatically since the late nineteenth century. The mountain lies within Kilimanjaro National Park, established in 1973 and designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987. Its climate and vegetation change markedly with altitude, from cultivated farmland and rainforest at the base to alpine desert and arctic-like conditions near the summit.
