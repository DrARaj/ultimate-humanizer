The Treaty of Versailles was the peace settlement that formally ended the state of war between Germany and the Allied Powers after the First World War. Negotiated at the Paris Peace Conference in 1919 and signed on 28 June in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles, it was drawn up largely without German participation. Its terms required Germany to accept responsibility for the war under Article 231, pay substantial reparations, cede territory including Alsace-Lorraine to France and land for the reconstituted Poland, relinquish its overseas colonies, and accept strict military limits, including a 100,000-man army and a demilitarized Rhineland. The treaty also created the League of Nations, though the United States Senate never ratified the agreement. Many contemporaries, most prominently John Maynard Keynes, criticized the settlement as punitive and economically damaging, and historians continue to debate whether its harshness fostered the resentment that fueled the rise of National Socialism and the Second World War.
