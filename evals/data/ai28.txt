Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920) was an Indian mathematician whose largely self-taught work produced striking results in number theory, infinite series and continued fractions. Born in Erode in the Madras Presidency, he worked as a clerk in Madras while filling notebooks with thousands of formulas, many without proof. In 1913 he sent a letter of his findings to the Cambridge mathematician G. H. Hardy, who recognised their originality and invited him to England. Ramanujan joined Trinity College, Cambridge, in 1914 and collaborated with Hardy on results including the Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic formula for the partition function. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1918 and became the first Indian Fellow of Trinity College. Ill health led him to return to India in 1919, and he died at Kumbakonam on 26 April 1920, aged 32. His notebooks, studied ever since, continue to inspire research, including work on mock theta functions.
